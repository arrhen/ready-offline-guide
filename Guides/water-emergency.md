## Before you start
Only use water that has been properly disinfected for drinking, cooking, making any prepared drink, washing dishes and brushing teeth.
In an emergency where regular water service has been interrupted, such as a hurricane, flood or water pipe breakage, local authorities may recommend using only bottled water, boiled water or disinfected water until regular service is restored.
Boiling or disinfection kills most disease-causing microorganisms that may be present in the water. It does not destroy other contaminants, such as heavy metals, salts and most other chemicals.

## Best choice: bottled or stored water
Use bottled water or water you have properly prepared and stored as an emergency water supply. If you have neither, boil the water.

## Cloudy water
Disinfection does not work as well when water is cloudy or colored. If water is cloudy, let it settle. Then filter it through a clean cloth, paper towel or coffee filter. Store the settled and filtered water in clean containers with covers.

## Boiling water
Boil water if you do not have bottled water. Boiling is sufficient to kill pathogenic bacteria, viruses and protozoa.
If the water is cloudy, let it settle and filter it through a clean cloth, paper towel or coffee filter.
Bring the water to a rolling boil for at least one minute. At altitudes above 5,000 feet (1,000 meters), boil the water for three minutes.
Let the water cool naturally and store it in clean containers with covers.
To improve the flat taste of boiled water, add one pinch of salt to each quart or liter of water, or pour the water from one clean container to another several times.

## Disinfecting water with household bleach
Use household bleach if you cannot boil water. Only use regular, unscented chlorine bleach products that are suitable for disinfection and sanitization as indicated on the label. The label may say that the active ingredient contains 6 or 8.25% of sodium hypochlorite. Do not use scented, color safe, or bleaches with added cleaners.
If water is cloudy, let it settle and filter it through a clean cloth, paper towel or coffee filter.
Find a clean dropper. Use fresh liquid chlorine bleach, or liquid chlorine bleach that has been stored at room temperature for less than one year.
Add 8 drops of 6% bleach, or 6 drops of 8.25% bleach, to each gallon of water. Double the amount of bleach if the water is cloudy, colored or very cold.
Stir and let stand for 30 minutes. The water should have a slight chlorine odor. If it does not, repeat the dosage and let stand for another 15 minutes before use.
If the chlorine taste is too strong, pour the water from one clean container to another and let it stand for a few hours before use.

## Bleach amounts by volume
Bleach may contain 6 or 8.25% sodium hypochlorite.
1 quart or liter: 2 drops of 6% bleach, 2 drops of 8.25% bleach.
1 gallon: 8 drops of 6% bleach, 6 drops of 8.25% bleach.
2 gallons: 16 drops (1/4 teaspoon) of 6% bleach, 12 drops (1/8 teaspoon) of 8.25% bleach.
4 gallons: 1/3 teaspoon of 6% bleach, 1/4 teaspoon of 8.25% bleach.
8 gallons: 2/3 teaspoon of 6% bleach, 1/2 teaspoon of 8.25% bleach.
Double the amount if the water is cloudy, colored or very cold.

## Water disinfection tablets
You can disinfect water with tablets that contain chlorine, iodine, chlorine dioxide or other disinfecting agents. These tablets are available online or at pharmacies and sporting goods stores. Follow the instructions on the product label, as each product may have a different strength.

## Other sources of water
Although bottled water is your best choice, you may be able to find other sources of water in and around your home, such as melting ice cubes or draining your hot water tank or pipes.
You can also use river or lake water. It is generally better to use flowing water than still, stagnant water. Do not use water with floating material in it, or water that has a dark color or questionable odor. Regardless of the source, treat the water first.
If you have a well on your property that has been flooded, disinfect and test the well water after the flood. Contact your state or local health department for advice.
